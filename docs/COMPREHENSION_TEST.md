# Economic Thread comprehension test

## Goal

Check whether a reader with no economics coursework can explain the thread accurately after one pass.

## Five-question script

1. What changed in prices from August to September?
2. Why might faster price growth matter for interest rates?
3. What happened to the 10-year government borrowing rate and the Nasdaq at the market open?
4. In your own words, how can higher interest rates affect future company profits?
5. Who may benefit and who may pay more when rates rise?

## Follow-up prompts

- Which arrow means the events happened in that order?
- Which arrow shows a common link rather than proven cause?
- What does the mortgage example illustrate, and what does it not predict?
- Name one other factor that can move markets.

## Pass criteria

- At least four of five core answers are substantially correct.
- The reader distinguishes sequence from a possible mechanism.
- The reader does not describe the diagram as proof that one event caused another.
- The reader identifies loans as potentially more expensive and some savings as potentially better paid.
- The reader can find the source link and the more precise explanation without help.

## Revision rule

If two readers miss the same question or flag the same term, revise the visible wording first. Add detail to the popover only when the short visible wording cannot stay accurate on its own.
